Hybrid Physics + Machine Learning for Optimizing High-Voltage Impulse Generator Settings 
Problem Overview 
High-voltage insulators used in power transmission systems must undergo impulse testing to 
verify their ability to withstand lightning strikes and switching surges. During these tests, engineers 
use a multi-stage impulse generator to produce standard lightning and switching impulse 
waveforms. 
A major challenge is selecting the correct generator settings. The required resistor values, 
charging voltages, and stage configurations depend on several factors such as: 
• Insulator capacitance 
• Measuring system capacitance 
• Stray capacitances in the test setup 
• Physical arrangement of the test equipment 
 
Currently, engineers often rely on calculations followed by multiple trial runs to obtain a waveform 
that satisfies standard requirements. This process can be time-consuming and may require 
repeated adjustments. 
The goal of this challenge is to develop an intelligent software tool that combines engineering 
calculations with machine learning to recommend suitable generator settings before physical 
testing begins.  
Challenge Statement 
Develop a software solution that predicts and recommends suitable impulse generator settings 
for high-voltage insulator testing. 
The solution should: 
• Support both lightning impulses (1.2/50 µs) and switching impulses (250/2500 µs). 
• Estimate suitable:  
o Front resistance 
o Tail resistance 
o Charging voltage 
o Number of active generator stages 
• Predict expected waveform characteristics. 
• Verify whether predicted waveforms satisfy specified tolerance limits. 
• Rank multiple feasible settings and recommend the best options. 
• Provide engineers with a reliable starting point before conducting actual tests.  
 
Expected Solution 
Participants are expected to build a software proof-of-concept, preferably a web application. 
The system should include: 
1. Engineering Calculation Module 
Uses circuit equations and input parameters to identify physically feasible operating conditions.

Additional Guidance: 
In practical impulse testing, the electrical parameters of the test circuit are not fixed. The test-
object/load capacitance, measuring-system/divider capacitance, stray capacitance, 
stray/connection inductance, connecting leads, physical arrangement and grounding/return-path 
configuration may vary depending on the test object and test setup. These variations can 
significantly influence the generated impulse waveform and, consequently, the optimum 
generator settings. 
Therefore, the proposed software should consider these variable and configuration-dependent 
parameters while recommending the generator configuration. 
In addition, the optimization should consider the actual available impulse-generator parameters 
and operating constraints, including: 
• Maximum charging voltage 
• Number of available stages 
• Stage voltage 
• Stored energy per stage and total stored energy 
• Impulse capacitance per stage 
• Available front resistors 
• Tail resistors for lightning and switching impulses 
• Charging resistor 
• External front/tail resistors, wherever applicable 
• Potential and discharging resistors 
• Basic/load capacitance of the generator 
• Sphere-gap dimensions and configuration 
• Available generator connection/configuration possibilities
